A policy decides how candidates fill the budget. The three built-in ones share one signature,
so the [eval](/astromesh/context/eval/) compares them as equals, and you can write a fourth
(see [docs/extending.md](https://github.com/monaccode/astromesh-context/blob/main/docs/extending.md)).
A policy is a pure function: the same candidates always give the same window.

| Policy | What it does | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| `ScoredKnapsack` | One budget, calibrated scores, caps, minimums and floors per source; `beta` adds value-per-token weighting (default 0) | The default |
| `FixedSlots` | A fixed share of the budget per source, filled in priority order | The baseline everyone runs today |
| `Everything` | Every candidate, or a clear error if they do not fit (`truncate=True` fills by score instead) | The control: what "just stuff it all in" costs |

```python
from astromesh_context import Everything, FixedSlots, LLMScorer, ScoredKnapsack, openai_compatible

knapsack = ScoredKnapsack(beta=0.0, min_score=0.1)
slots = FixedSlots({"okf": 0.3, "rag": 0.5, "history": 0.2})
control = Everything(truncate=True)

# LLMScorer is a scoring step, not a policy: pass it as ContextEngine(..., scorer=scorer)
complete = openai_compatible("https://api.example.com/v1", "api-key", "model-name")
scorer = LLMScorer(complete, top_n=30, alpha=0.5, timeout=10.0)
```

`LLMScorer` works with any policy. It needs the `llm` extra and any OpenAI-compatible endpoint.
The values in the snippet are the ones the library documents: `beta=0` (the default), a score
floor of 0.1, and a scorer that rescores the top 30 candidates, blends at `alpha=0.5` and gives
up after 10 seconds.

## What happens before the policy

1. **Calibrate.** A cosine of 0.82, a BM25 score of 14 and "the third most recent turn" are not
   comparable. Scores are normalized by rank within each source, then weighted by how much you
   trust that source (`weight` in the source config). Signals adjust them where you can see it
   in the trace: a stale concept scores lower (x0.7), a human-reviewed one higher (x1.1), a
   deprecated one is left out.
2. **Score with a model (optional).** `LLMScorer` rescores the top candidates in one batched
   call and blends its score with the calibrated one. If it fails, times out or returns
   something unparseable, the window is built on the calibrated scores and the trace records
   `scorer_fallback:<reason>`.
3. **Deduplicate.** Exact copies by hash, near-copies by containment of 5-word shingles: the
   overlap divided by the size of the smaller text, at 0.8 or more. A retrieval chunk is
   recognized as part of the concept it came from. The copy that stays inherits the dropped
   one's signals, so a RAG chunk of a policy that OKF marks deprecated goes out with it.
   Conversation history is never deduplicated.

## How `ScoredKnapsack` fills the budget

Pinned items (the system prompt, the OKF root index) go in first. Then a minimum per source
(`min_items`; the last user turn, always), then the rest by score, respecting a cap per source
(`max_tokens`) and a score floor (`min_score`). If the pinned items alone exceed the budget, the
build fails with `BudgetExceededError` and the per-item token counts, rather than trimming them
silently.

`beta` weights the score by value per token. It defaults to 0 because on the published corpus it
lost at tight budgets (recall 0.267 against 0.550 at 750 tokens); see
[Eval](/astromesh/context/eval/).

`FixedSlots` takes a share of the budget per source name and fills each in priority order. An
item that does not fit in its source's share is excluded with `share_exhausted`.

<Aside type="note" title="What the knapsack adds">
On the published level-1 run, `ScoredKnapsack` ties `Everything` truncated on recall, precision
and distractor rate, because both fill by score order. What the knapsack adds over truncation is
caps, minimums and floors per source, not a better ranking.
</Aside>

## Order after the policy

Stable items first in a fixed order, then session items, then volatile ones, with the strongest
at the edges of their block, where models read best. History stays chronological and last.
Each item declares its `stability` (`stable`, `session` or `volatile`); stable content goes
first and is byte-identical across calls, so the prompt cache can reuse the longest possible
prefix.
